About

I’m a Group Product Manager at BrowserStack, where I lead platform, data, and analytics products. My world is the backend data platform, the computation engine, and the visualization layers that quietly power products generating more than $140M in annual recurring revenue. I like problems that live where rigorous engineering meets real business outcomes.

I joined BrowserStack as an Associate Product Manager in 2020 and grew into a Group Product Manager — the only PM at the company promoted four times in under five years, and a recipient of the BrowserStack CEO Hall of Fame award. Before product, I spent five years building mission-critical software for nuclear reactors and real-time operating system kernels for the government. That foundation shaped how I think: correctness, performance, and stability are not afterthoughts.

I’m a deeply technical PM. I partner closely with engineering on the things that decide whether a product holds up under load — data ingestion pipelines with Kafka, data architecture, and database schema choices — and just as closely with go-to-market teams, value-selling to executives and speaking to audiences of 500 or more. I hold an MBA from IIM Bangalore, and an M.Tech and B.Tech in Computer Science & Engineering.

What I’ve done

  • Built BrowserStack’s Test Reporting & Analytics product from scratch, scaling it from zero to 30,000+ monthly active customers with a +50 NPS.
  • Grew test automation framework support — Cypress, Playwright, Puppeteer — now used by customers contributing roughly $20M in ARR.
  • Led a performance initiative across 19 global data centers (a 2-core to 4-core upgrade plus software optimizations) that improved test execution speed by 30% and cut deals lost to performance from 20% to under 3%.
  • Overhauled the developer documentation platform, lifting documentation satisfaction from 23% to 78%, roughly doubling the new-user “aha moment” rate, and driving a ~40% increase in paid conversions.
  • Lead a team of product managers accountable for the work of multiple engineering squads — 35+ engineers in all.

How I think

I believe a great product manager should be a true technical partner to engineering, not someone standing at a distance from it. I treat documentation as a product in its own right, because clear docs drive bottom-up, product-led growth. I see data stability and performance as business-critical metrics, not just engineering concerns. And I like to pressure-test architecture against concrete, user-facing requirements — the design only matters if it holds up where real people meet the product.
