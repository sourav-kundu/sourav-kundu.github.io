About

Engineer at heart, product leader by craft.

I’m a Group Product Manager at BrowserStack, where I lead the platform, data, and analytics layer that quietly powers products generating more than $140M in annual recurring revenue. My world runs from the data a client SDK emits, through real-time Kafka ingestion pipelines, to the database schemas and the visualization layer users actually see. I like problems that live where rigorous engineering meets real business outcomes.

I started out in Alipurduar, a small town in West Bengal, and made my way to an M.Tech in Computer Science at BARC, five years building mission-critical software for nuclear reactors, an MBA from IIM Bangalore (top 10% of the batch), and then BrowserStack. I’m the only PM in the company’s history promoted four times in under five years, from Associate PM to Group PM, and a recipient of the CEO Hall of Fame.

That engineering foundation shapes how I work: correctness, performance, and stability are first-class product concerns, not afterthoughts. I partner with engineering on the decisions that determine whether a product holds up under load, and just as closely with go-to-market teams — value-selling to executives and speaking to rooms of 500+ engineers. I own product analytics end-to-end, too: I define the instrumentation and the metrics, then measure them myself in Amplitude or hand-written BigQuery SQL, rather than waiting on a data team. In the last few months I’ve gone further and become a full-stack builder myself, shipping production features and an entire “PM Operating System” with AI coding tools.

How I think

Operating principles I keep coming back to.

A PM should be a true technical partner.

Not standing at a distance from engineering — in the data models, the schema choices, the architecture. I pressure-test designs against concrete, user-facing requirements, because a design only matters if it holds up where real people meet the product.

Stability and performance are business metrics.

I’ve proven to a CTO that low data stability has catastrophic, customer-facing impact. Reliability isn’t an engineering nicety you bolt on later; it’s a number the business should care about and I should own.

Build the thing, then prove it pays.

From a single-handed ROI calculator that closes executive buyers, to dashboards I build myself — I’d rather demonstrate value concretely than argue it abstractly. Measure, instrument, and hold teams accountable to the number.

Win the battle in front of you.

I persevere until I do. I like hard, ambiguous problems where the right answer isn’t obvious — and I don’t want a role far from the engineering and the business. The closer to where the work actually happens, the better.
